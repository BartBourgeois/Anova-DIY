> I work in IT, so let me explain why this case is hard to accept.
>
> This cooker didn't fail from age or use. It was working perfectly until it ran an update supplied through your own app. A firmware update that stalls at 75% and leaves a device with no beep, no display and no lights means there is no fallback in the update process. Even $10 smart plugs handle this properly: they write the new image to a second partition (A/B), verify it, and keep booting the previous working firmware if the update fails or is interrupted. A $450 professional-grade device should be at least as robust as that.
>
> Since my last message, and after it had already failed, I've opened the unit and started investigating it myself. The main board (ANOVA_PRO_MAIN_V2.0) is built around a TI CC3220S, which only boots a firmware image signed by Anova. So I can read and back up the flash myself, but I can't restore working firmware without Anova's help.
>
> Any of the following would solve this for me, in order of preference:
>
> 1. The signed firmware image for my Precision Cooker Pro (AN600-20, S/N [serial number]), plus any flashing or recovery instructions you can share, so I can reflash it myself. Because the image is signed, it can only put the cooker back on stock firmware: any modification would break the signature. I'm comfortable with electronics, accept that this is entirely at my own risk, and won't hold Anova responsible for the outcome. This costs Anova nothing.

> 3. A mail-in repair, so Anova can reflash the board, at cost if needed.

## 9 · 6 Nov 2026, 23:39 · raptox → Anova support
*Ticket stamp: Nov 6, 2026, 2:39 PM PST. No attachment.*

> Hi [agent],
>
> Thanks for the procedure, and sorry for the slow reply.
>
> I did try it, but on a panel that doesn't light up at all, it had nothing to work with.
>
> Since none of the four options I asked for on 1 November came through, I went ahead on my own.
>
> I'm happy to say the cooker works again: it's back on Wi-Fi and in the app, and its first test cook held 54 °C right on point.
>
> In short:
>
> the interrupted update most likely left the new firmware half-written. Every time the cooker was switched on after that, its Wi-Fi chip found the unfinished firmware and counted it as a security problem.
>
> After enough restarts, the chip's built-in safety lock tripped, and once that happens it refuses to start until it gets fresh firmware.
>
> That's why there was no sign of life at all, and why waiting or unplugging it could never help.
>
> Nothing was actually broken. The board, the power supply and the rest of the hardware were all fine.
>
> Finding that out took me about a week: opening the cooker, mapping the board under a microscope, making a full backup of both chips, and practising every step on a test board first.
>
> The repair itself turned out to be simple.
>
> I put the current firmware back on through the chip's own service connection, unmodified, together with the cooker's own settings from my backup.
>
> That took 26 seconds.
>
> I'll admit I enjoyed the puzzle, but it's also why this has been a bit frustrating.
>
> With the firmware file and a page of instructions, this would have been an evening's work for me.
>
> A mail-in reflash would have taken your service team a few minutes.
>
> Instead it took a week and a lot of resources/hardware/software...still incredible.
>
> I'm still confirming the cause.
>
> f it holds, it's worth passing on to your firmware team: an update interrupted at the wrong moment can lock a Pro without anything being broken, and the same simple fix brings it back.
>
> I'm happy to share the details with them.
>
> There's nothing more needed on this ticket, so feel free to close it.
>
> Thanks for your time, [agent]!
>
> Both my Pros are cooking again.
>
> Kind regards,
>
> [raptox]
