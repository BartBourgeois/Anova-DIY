## Next: microscope survey (first job when the Andonstar AD409 Pro-ES arrives)
Goal: one **stitched, measurable mosaic** per board side to replace the phone photo as the reference, plus sharp
close-ups of every marking and debug footprint.

### Sets, in order
Since  every board is shot on both sides, each side with a ruler frame, once with top light and once with
backlight. A back mosaic is then mirrored and registered on features seen from both sides (board outline, J29 and
JP5 holes, locating pins), so a via can be followed from one side to the other.
1. **Main board, component side** (raptox's unit): full mosaic. **Done 2026-11-03** (top light, see above); the
   backlight pass and a gap re-shoot are still to do.
2. **Close-ups** at higher magnification, not stitched:
   - markings that are partly illegible now: U17, U2, U13, U14
   - J14 pads, J29, J37/J38 straight on, TP groups
   - laser-etched markings: take each one twice, with diffuse light and with light from the side
   - **layer-count evidence**, all non-destructive:
     - the board edge at the break-off tab marks: look for copper lines in the cross-section
     - the RF traces from U1 and U7 to their antennas (width and gap to ground, with the ruler frame)
     - any fab or layer markings
     - **backlight** with the Elgato Key Light:
       - **Setup:**
         - unplug the FFC at J8 and the speaker at J9
         - lay the bezel + board assembly bezel-down on the panel. The board is heat-staked to the bezel, but the white
           light guide passes light (the segment walls will cast shadows).
         - Key Light at full brightness, microscope lights off, room dark, exposure locked
       - **Method:** shoot each spot twice without moving the board, with top light and with backlight. Copper on any
         layer shows as a shadow. A spot that is dark in backlight but has no copper on the component side, nor in the
         FCC LED-side photo, has inner copper.
       - It is transillumination, not X-ray: chips and other parts block the light.
3. **Power board, both sides**, if it can be removed (unplugged, see the mains rule). Its ICs (U2, U10, ...) are unknown,
   and one may be an MCU.
4. Main board LED side: only if the board ever comes off the bezel. Not needed now.

### Capture protocol (mosaic sets)
- **Board:**
  - unpowered, flat on a matte background, wrist strap on
  - move the **board**, not the microscope
  - slide it along a straightedge so the rotation stays constant
- **Grid:** about **40 % overlap** in both directions, row by row in a serpentine order.
- **Settings:**
  - one magnification and one focus setting for the whole set. Refocusing also shifts and rescales the image
    (Elektor review), so it breaks the stitch.
  - lock exposure and white balance if the menu allows it, and keep the same light for every tile
- **Scale:** first frame of each set = a mm ruler lying in the board plane, at the same settings. It gives mm/px for the
  whole set.
- **Resolution:** the menu goes up to 5600 x 4200 ("24M"), but the sensor is 4 MP, so that is interpolated (likely,
  per the manual's specs). On arrival, shoot one frame at each setting and compare the detail. Use the smallest setting
  that loses nothing.
- **Saving settings:** the device only keeps its settings if you switch it off with a long press on the power button.
  Pulling the power loses them (manual, FAQ 3).
- **Depth of field and parallax:** choose a magnification where the chips and pads are sharp. Tall parts (caps,
  connectors, inductors) will blur and lean between tiles because the lens is not telecentric. That is expected, and the
  close-ups cover them.
- **What the PC can control** (source: AD409 Pro user manual Ver 01,
  <https://electrokit.com/upload/quick/1a/a6/873e_AD409-pro_users_manual-Ver_01_1.pdf>; the Pro-ES is the same unit plus
  the endoscope, likely):
  - **Manual only:**
    - magnification = height of the monitor on the column
    - focus = the knob
    - the two base lights
  - **Zoom+/- buttons:** digital zoom only. Leave them at 1x for mosaics.
  - Everything known about the microscope, its outputs and its quirks: [microscope.md](microscope.md).
  - **USB "PC Camera" mode** (tried 2026-11-03):
    - select it on the microscope after plugging in the data cable (not the power cable)
    - Windows lists it as "USB Camera" (USB VID 0603, PID 8612) on its built-in UVC driver: a standard webcam,
      `confirmed`. [`tools/microscope/capture.py`](../tools/microscope/README.md) saves frames from it without
      Andonstar's "Microscope Measure" software.
    - **The live view tops out at 1920x1080** (MJPG or H.264, 30 fps; also 1280x720, 640x480, 320x240): `confirmed`,
      from the modes the driver advertises. So USB frames are 2 MP. Full-resolution tiles have to come from the
      microSD card (way A, or way B with an SD trigger).
    - **A mode it doesn't advertise hangs its video stream** until the USB cable is replugged: `confirmed` with
      3840x2160 (the stream died on the request, and every open after it timed out until the replug).
    - Which camera controls (exposure, WB) are exposed over USB: still unknown.
  - **Wi-Fi (the manual calls it a test function):**
    - needs a microSD card inserted; app "Ucam Dashcam"; access point `Andonstar-…`, default password 12345678
    - a dashcam app suggests a dashcam SoC. If it is a Novatek one, there is a known HTTP API (`cmd=1001` = take a photo,
      plus file listing and download): guess.
    - Probe it only with read-only requests. Never send format, delete or settings commands.
  - **IR remote:** the microscope has an IR receiver. A Pico with an IR LED (and an IR receiver module to learn the
    codes) can press "capture" from the PC. This is the fallback for triggering full-resolution shots to the SD card.
- **Ways to shoot:**
  - **A:** raptox shoots to the microSD card.
  - **B:** capture assistant. It watches the USB live view and raptox slides the board. When the overlap is about 40 % and
    the image is still and sharp, it either saves the frame or triggers a full-resolution SD shot (over Wi-Fi or IR). It
    also keeps a coverage map, so no gaps. The SD files are imported byte-identical afterwards.

### Storage and stitching (originals never edited, blobs never committed)
- **Tiles are originals, kept out of git** (decided 2026-11-03; a set is about 60 MB of tiles plus a
  mosaic of several hundred MB):
  - copy the stitcher's set folder byte-identical to the gitignored `micro/<date>_<set>/`, check every tile against the
    SHA-256 in its `session.json`, and keep a copy off the machine
  - commit the set's `session.json` and `layout.json` as `images/mosaics/<date>_<set>.{session,layout}.json`
  - log one row per set here: board/side, tiles, capture, light, scale, stitch quality, hashes
- **The mosaic is generated** by the separate stitcher project and stays in `micro/`. Board images and
  close-ups come from it through `crops.json` entries with `"local": true` and `max_size`; anything that must not be
  published gets a `redact` box there.
- **The full resolution goes on the site**, with the same `redact` boxes:
  `py -3.13 tools/deepzoom.py <date>_<set> --png <date>_<set>_mosaic.png` writes the viewer's tiles to
  `images/mosaics/` (committed) and the lossless PNG, which goes up as an asset of the `mosaics` GitHub release.
  Then add a section to [mosaics.md](mosaics.md).
- **Check every mosaic** against known sizes: pad pitches (0.5 mm on U1, U7, J8; 2.54 mm on J29, JP5), U1 body 9 mm.
  Also look at the seams that cross chips. Measure only board-level features: tall parts lean.
