# J8: the 30-pin link between the main board and the power board

Every one of the 30 pins gets a destination on each board, a function, a direction, a level and its evidence. The
table fills as measurements come in. It describes the original boards, and it is also the specification for a main
board of our own (roadmap stage 6). Other main-board nets are in [nets.md](nets.md).

- **Connector:** J8 on both boards, 30-way FPC, 0.5 mm pitch, flip actuator (main board: [callouts](callouts/main_component-side_micro.md)).
- **Pin 1** is the end with the printed circle on the main board; `30` is printed at the other end (`likely` from the
  silkscreen; the measurement below agrees).
- **Cable:** the cooker's own FFC, same-side contacts (type A, `likely` from the photos), so pin n meets pin n.
  For the bench: QUARKZMAN `FPC-30P 0.5MM` break-out A with a 100 mm type A FFC
  ([bench setup](bench-setup.md#step-1b-bench-supply-normal-way-of-working-afterwards)).
- **Header mapping on the bench:** break-out header pin n = J8 pin n (`likely`). The six supply pins come out on
  header pins 1-6; a reversed cable would have put them on 25-30. In the [bench photo](../../images/own/2026-11-04/01_bench_main-board_breakout-A-on-J8.jpg)
  the cable also runs straight, 30 end to 30 end. To close it: header pins 25-30 are open to JP5 pin 1.
  On break-out B, on the cooker's own FFC, the power board's supply arrives on header pins 1-6 and returns on 30, so header n = J8 n there too (`likely`).
- **Supply: 5.01 V** (`confirmed`, measured 2026-11-04): pins 1-6 to pin 30 at break-out B, on the cooker's
  own FFC, with the cooker live and the **main board unplugged**, so without load
  ([photo](../../images/own/2026-11-04/05_bench_J8-supply_5V01_on-breakout-B.jpg)). Pins 1-6 feed U17's input directly
  (continuity to C577's pad, `confirmed`), and U17 accepts at most 6 V ([nets](nets.md#supply)). Under load:
  not measured yet.
- **Isolation from mains:** the AC check from GND to mains earth isn't logged yet (bench setup step 1a).
- **Ground:** pins 10, 11, 17, 19, 22, 23 and 30 ring solid to JP5 pin 2, and JP5 pin 2 rings to U15 pin 4: **GND**
  (`confirmed`/). Seven pins, spread between the signal pins.
- **JP5** is the board's power header: pin 1 = pins 1-6 = U17's input, pin 2 = GND, no short between them
  (`confirmed`).

## What the photos suggested, and what the meter said
From the top layer of the main-board mosaic (`guess` per pin), checked against the measurements above:
- **GND candidates** before the meter: 7, 9-12, 18, 20, 22?, 24, 30 (no copper visible on the top layer). The meter found 10, 11, 22 and 30 on JP5 pin 2's net, but 7, 9, 12, 18, 20 and 24 not. Pins 17, 19 and 23, read as
  signals to vias, are on that net too: their vias drop straight to it. Pins with no visible copper and no ground (7, 9, 12,
  18, 20) may be not connected at all.
- **Signals with long top-layer traces:** 8 and 13-16, running up-left toward the chips. Traced on 2026-11-04: only 8, 13 and 14 leave as traces, and they run to U7's corner; 15 and 16 show none on the top layer.
- **Signals to vias** within 1-2 mm of the connector: 21, 25, 26, 28, 29 (and 27?).
  Followed on 2026-11-12: 25, 26 and 29 each end in their own via, 27 and 28 share one, 24 has no
  top-layer copper, and the GND pins 22 and 23 share a via.

## Open pins: what must cross, and where it can be
State on 2026-11-12. On the main board: 13 pins `confirmed` (supply 1-6, seven GND), 3 `likely`
(8, 13, 14 to U7), **14 pins open, 13 nets** (27 and 28 are tied). On the power board: **no pin traced yet**. The
supply is measured without load only (5.01 V).

### The signals that must cross
The Bluetooth chip U7 (CC2640R2F) is the cooker's real-time controller: it drives the heater and the pump and reads
the sensors, and J8 is its only way to them. The list below comes from the unit's own firmware, read offline (the
notes are not published); every row is `likely` until a measurement on the cable agrees.

| Signal | U7 pin | Dir (seen from the main board) | Character | J8 pin |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| pump drive | 11 (DIO_6) | out | PWM, period 128 µs (about 7.8 kHz), active high; the original runs it between about 3 % and 75 %; low from reset and when idle | open |

| water level: critical | 31 (DIO_21) | in | level, no pull; high = water below this sensor | **13** (`likely`) |

All of it `likely` from the firmware until the cable agrees. "No pull" means U7 adds no pull
resistor: the power board (or a resistor on the main board) must hold each flag line at a defined level.

That is 12 nets with the I2C pair (or 13-14 with analog lines), plus the tied pair 27/28 as one more; 16 nets are
available. So about three pins are spare, not connected, or carry something not found yet: a supply for the power
board's logic is a candidate (27/28). No serial protocol other than the converter's I2C crosses the cable: the
drives are raw PWM lines and the flags raw levels (`likely`). The Wi-Fi chip U1 has no known line to J8.

### What the main board does with them
The Wi-Fi chip U1 runs both control loops and the protections; U7 only applies the two duties it is sent, reads
the lines and passes them on (from the firmware, `likely`; details in the private firmware notes):
- **Heater:** a PI loop on the water temperature, once a second, with a soft start (at most a quarter of full power
  for the first 5 s after a new set-point) and derating: the allowed power drops by an eighth per degree above 97 °C
  at the heater or the triac (zero at 105 °C), and above 72 °C on a chip temperature it also reads.
- **Pump:** a speed loop on the pump-speed pulses, four times a second. Its target falls when the water level does
  (one step at "low", another at "critical"); with no pulses it restarts at a fixed duty.
- **Both drives go to zero** when idle, at low water (both level sensors dry for more than 0.1 s), at a water
  temperature of 98 °C or more, and on a leak or a blocked pump. The last two latch until a restart.
- **U7 on its own** acts on none of the flags. Its one protection: it sets the heater to zero 5 s after its last
  command from U1.

For an own main board: both drive lines low from power-up until the firmware runs, every flag line held at a defined
level, the converter reached over I2C, and these protections rebuilt (better: in hardware for the heater).

### How they get closed, cheapest and safest first
1. **Firmware, at the desk:** done for the pump-speed pin, the flags' levels, the converter and the control loops. Left: the temperature curves (the sensor values to expect on the meter).
2. **The board from U7 outward:** U7 pins 5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 17 and 28 followed on the mosaic, then one unpowered
   meter session: the three `likely` traces beeped, those seven U7 pins against the open header pins, 27/28 against
   each other and the 3.3 V rail, and the measurements listed below.
3. **The power board, cold** (mains plug out), through the break-out on the cooker's own cable: ohms and diode mode
   on every open pin, and the temperature lines with the tube in cold, then warm water.
4. **The main board alone on the bench supply** with the logic analyser: which pins it drives, and how the two
   drive lines behave from power-up. Nothing is on mains.
5. **The power board live, battery meter only:** the isolation from mains first, then each pin's idle level.
6. **The live tap:** the [FFC30 tap](../../hardware/Raptox-FFC30-Tap/README.md) between both boards, the logic
   analyser on a laptop running on battery, one capture per situation (power-up, idle, pump, heating, low water).
   Only after step 5's isolation result.

## Next measurements
Unpowered, from the break-out header:
Done: JP5 pin 2 to U15 pin 4, JP5 pin 1 to U17 pin 7/8, JP5 1-2 no short, pins 24-26 to 3.3 V, VIN and each
other. GND is U15 pin 4: the `XMC` SOP-8 at the board edge next to the J14 footprint; its pin 1 is the leg at the
moulded dimple, next to two white silkscreen dots, pin 4 the far end of that row, pin 8 (3.3 V) across from pin 1 at
the dimple end of the other row (pin 5 is the one diagonally opposite pin 1; SOIC-8 numbering runs counter-clockwise).
1. Pins 24-26: the unit of the settled readings, the resistance with the leads swapped (a change means a semiconductor
   in the path), and diode mode both ways to GND (about 0.5-0.7 V one way means a chip pin's protection diode).
2. Every other header pin to 3.3 V (U15 pin 8) and to VIN (JP5 pin 1), with diode mode both ways to GND. No beep to
   either supply makes "signal or not connected" measured (working assumption since 2026-11-04). Open both
   ways in diode mode means not connected on the main board. A 0.4-0.7 V drop means a chip pin.
3. Optional before power: R589 and R590 (U17's output voltage). U17 pin 1/2 to U15 pin 8 is done.

Then the supply under power: about 5 V on JP5, with the pre-power checklist in
[bench setup](bench-setup.md#step-1b-bench-supply-normal-way-of-working-afterwards).
